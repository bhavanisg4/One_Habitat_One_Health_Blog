World AMR Awareness Week 2026

When Medicines Stop Working: Understanding Antimicrobial Resistance

Published November 18, 2026   |   Author: Dr. Manjunath Chimma, Master's in Veterinary Public Health

One Health, one action: Prevent AMR now.

Imagine having an infection, taking medicines and still not getting better. The reason could be antimicrobial resistance (AMR), a growing health concern that makes infections harder to treat.

Key idea: Antimicrobial resistance is a One Health issue. Human health, animal health and the environment are connected, so responsible action is needed across all three.

What is antimicrobial resistance?

AMR occurs when microorganisms such as bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites change over time and no longer respond as expected to medicines used to treat the infections they cause. As a result, infections can become more difficult to prevent and treat.

Why is AMR a One Health concern?

People, animals and the environment share microorganisms and ecosystems. The use and misuse of antimicrobial medicines, infection prevention practices, sanitation and environmental conditions can all influence the spread of resistant microorganisms.

What can we do?

Use antibiotics and other antimicrobial medicines only when prescribed by a qualified health professional. Follow the prescribed instructions, never share or use leftover medicines, maintain hygiene, support vaccination and infection prevention, and seek reliable veterinary advice for animal health concerns.

Conclusion

Preventing AMR requires coordinated action. Individuals, healthcare professionals, veterinarians, farmers, communities and policymakers all have a role in protecting the effectiveness of antimicrobial medicines.

Remember: Medicines work best when they are used responsibly. Small, informed actions can help protect health across generations.
